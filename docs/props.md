# Props and world assets

Rules for new props, and how each prop works (`src/world/props.js`, `src/world/stubbies.js`).
The physics engine, layers and the bird's solid are in `physics.md`.

## Rules for a new prop or set piece

- Code: a prop class takes `(physics, asset, def)`, with `def.at` (`[x, z]`), `def.heading` and `def.base` (the height of its bottom, from `place.js`). It has a static `footprint(def)` for the placement. Add it to `PROPS` in `src/world/kinds.js`. Then a level can list it. See `levels.md`.

- Bird scale: the ibis is 2 units tall (about 70 cm; 1 unit is about 35 cm), and its solid is 3 kg with a push of at most 150 N (`BIRD` in `physics.js`). Size and weigh every object for a bird of that size and strength, not for a person.
- Masses are game masses at bird scale. Pick them for how the object must act when the bird meets it:
  - Kicked about by the feet and the head: light layer, less than about 1 kg. Football 0.5, cereal box 0.3, stubby 1.0.
  - Shoved, slid by a walk, tipped by a run: heavy layer, about 1 to 4 kg. Cardboard box 1.2, wheelie bin 4 (lid 0.35).
  - Too big for a bird to move (a letterbox, a fence, a tree, a parked car): static, on the `terrain` or `fixed` layer.
  - Check it: walk and then run the bird into the prop (headless, in free play). A walk must not throw a light prop far, and a run must not throw a heavy one.
- Australian, not American: the street is an Australian suburb, and every object is Australian in style and product. Examples: wheelie bins (red lid general waste, yellow recycling, green garden), an AFL football, stubbies, a nature strip, a rolled kerb, brick letterboxes, a Hills Hoist, a corrugated iron fence, gum trees. Not: an American mailbox on a post, a fire hydrant, a yellow school bus, an American football, American road markings. Cars drive on the left.
- No real brands: take the general colours and shapes of a classic Australian product (as the stubbies after a gold lager bottle), with no logo or name.
- Cost (the GPU is the limit; the frame budget is in `environment.md`, "Frame cost"):
  - Draws: merge the parts of one rigid body with `toonParts()`, and share the prop materials. Many copies of one object: one instanced mesh (as `Stubbies`, the fence posts), not one mesh each.
  - Solid and opaque: write depth and keep renderOrder 0. `Level` then draws it before the ground (`BEFORE_GROUND` in `level.js`), so the ground shader skips the pixels under it. An object that writes no depth (a decal, a puddle) needs a renderOrder between -1 and 0, after the ground.
  - Triangles: low meshes. Each mesh draws about 3 times (toon, outline, shadow). See the counts in `improvements.md`.
  - Shaders: no `gl_FragDepth` (it turns off the early depth test). For noise, use `noise2()` (`src/world/noise.js`), not `noise3()`.
  - Shadows: `castShadow` only where the shadow reads. The props with `castShadow` also draw into the lamp shadow atlas.
  - Measure before and after with `npm run gpu` (`tools.md`), at day and at night.

## Props

- The bin and its lid share a collision group (`newGroup()`), so they don't collide. The hinge has a soft position spring: the lid opens with the tilt and the spin of the bin (`BIN.flap`). Gravity alone keeps a lid shut in most falls.
- Prop meshes: `toonParts(parts, asset.uniforms)` merges the parts of one rigid body (`[geometry, [lit, shade, line?], at]`) into one toon mesh and one outline, with the colours on each vertex (`tone()`), and all props share the two materials. A part with no line colour gets no outline. Draw calls in free play went from 143 to 81. A new part that moves alone (a lid, a flap) is its own `toonParts()` call.
- Rest: each prop keeps a `Rest` for its bodies. When every body has slept for one update and none is in a bill, `still()` is true and the prop skips its placing, slam check and hinge targets (a sleeping body ignores them), and the stubbies skip their matrix upload. A prop must clear `events` when it skips. A prop that replaces a body (`Cereal.squash()`) needs a new `Rest`. `Cereal.under()` still runs at rest: a foot can come down on a box before anything wakes it.
- Cardboard boxes (`Box`): 1.2 to 1.4 tall (60 to 70% of the bird), 1.2 kg, friction 0.5, on the heavy layer, as the bin: the bird shoves them, stands on them and jumps on top. Tape and a label are flat planes a little proud of the card. A box slam has `kind: 'box'`: a dull papery thud in `sound.js`, not the bin's plastic one.
- Perch: `perch()` on the bin and the boxes (`Tub`) gives the middle of the top, world, where a flying miner can land, or null when the tub tips more than `PERCH.tilt` or the lid or a flap is open. It casts two rays down, halfway to the front and to the back: a ray in the middle goes through the gap between the two box flaps and finds the floor of the box. `cap` is the thickness of the lid or the flaps.
- Box flaps: two thin bodies, each hinged on a long top edge (`addHinge`, the axis turns with the side so both open at less than 0), with the tub in one collision group. The target opens with the tilt and the spin (`CARTON.flap`), as the bin lid. The flap spring is stiffer than the lid's (4 Hz, not 1.5): a light flap on a weak spring never lifted against gravity. The box is hollow, as the bin (`CARTON.wall`).
- Cereal boxes (`Cereal`): 0.74 and 0.86 tall, 0.3 kg, on the light layer, as the ball: the feet and the head knock them over. Print: a title band and a round logo, front and back (`CEREAL.prints`). Slam `kind: 'cereal'`: a light card tap with a rattle.
- Squash: `Cereal.update(ibises)` (the demo passes the birds to every prop) tests each sole (the ankle and a point `crush.toes` ahead, `crush.width` round them) against the top of the box, and the bird's solid from above (`crush.body`: a box between the feet). Then `squash()` removes the body and adds a thin heavy one: a sheet `crush.thick` thick on the ground, front up, so the bird stands on it. The mesh only scales (`print.scale`). Event `{ type: 'squash' }`: puffs at the corners and a crunch (`sound.squash()`). A box stomped upright as a thin sliver read badly: the sheet is always flat.
- Stubbies (`Stubbies`): empty (no caps, an open mouth), 1.0 tall (half the bird), 1.0 kg, light layer, in a triangle of `STUBBY.rows` on the road. Hull from the lathe outline with the mouth closed (`HULL`), centred on the middle so `Slam` fits. Slam `kind: 'glass'`: a clink, no dust.
- Switches (`Physics.addSwitch(box, turn)`): a world box that a peck turns in place of a pick up (the sprinkler's tap). `nearest()` and `target()` take the switches with the grabbable props (`targets()`), so the nearest of all wins, and the highlight shows it. A switch is not a body: nothing collides with it.
- Grab: the peck sends `grab` on the way down in its first jab (t 0.08). `Physics.grab()` picks up the nearest grabbable prop (`grab: { hold, edge }` on `addHull()` or `addBox()`, light only: the stubbies and the cereal boxes) whose AABB is within `GRAB.reach` of the bill tip, so the aim can be rough. The prop pops into the bill at once, crosswise (its y along the head's x, its x out of the bill, its z across the gap), kinematic on the `ghost` layer (it touches nothing), moved each step in `placeLimbs()`. `GRAB.holds` gives each kind of prop its bill opening: `round` (a stubby, 0.4 across) and `flat` (a cereal box, 0.2 thick). Each has `jaw`, `grip` (head space, the middle of the gap at that jaw) and `out` (the direction out of the gap). The bill curves down, so a wider jaw moves the gap down and back: change the three together, from the bill shapes in `model.glsl`. `edge` moves the prop out of the bill, so the bill holds a box by its side edge (`CEREAL.bite`). `controller.carry` (the jaw of the hold, or null) keeps the bill open. A peck while the bird holds a prop plays `drop` (`Character.act()`): no jab, the bill opens to `GRIP.drop`, and `drop` makes the prop dynamic on `light` again. No slam clink while held. With no grabbable prop in reach but another dynamic prop (`bulky`: the ball, the boxes, the bin), `grab()` plays `fumble`: the bill opens wide over the prop (the head stays high, or it goes into the prop), snaps shut once, then the bird gives up. The check is early in the jab, so the fumble takes over before the bill goes into the prop.
- Pick-up: a grab that takes a prop plays `lift` in place of the rest of the peck: the head comes straight up with the prop and tosses a little (0.4 s; the peck alone has two jabs, 1.7 s). The legs are not in its mask, so the feet ease back from the peck stance as the peck fades out.
- Highlight (`highlight.js`): a white outline round the prop that a peck would pick up now. `Physics.target(ibis)` uses the test of `grab()` (`nearest()`) at `peckTip()`: where the bill tip is at the `grab` event, from a scratch `Character` run through a peck, so it follows the peck clip. A grabbable prop gives `hull(id)` (outline geometry and world matrix). Draws, before the glass: a stencil mask of the prop's faces (no depth test: the label is in front of the glass) and of its dark line where it shows, then the white hull outside the mask. The white takes the depth of the front of the prop's bounding sphere (`outlineMaterial(..., { front })`): at the depth of its back faces, the ground in front of a fallen bottle covered the white below it. It writes depth, so the glass of a bottle behind does not draw over it (after the glass, the glass stencil cut the rim there). Width: `HIGHLIGHT.screen`, a band of that part of the view height past the dark line (`outlineMaterial(..., { screen })`), so it reads at any distance, and the dark line, wider up close, does not hide it.
- Stubby look: golden amber glass, a plain yellow body label and neck collar (`BANDS`), after a classic Australian stubby, with no brand. A label with bands read as too busy. The middle of the glass glows a little (`GLASS.glow`).
- Stubby draws, all instanced: the label bands (one opaque mesh, `aLit` and `aShade` on each vertex: `toonMaterial(..., { tones: true })`), then, transparent and in `renderOrder`, the glass (front faces only, a toon highlight) and the outline. The glass shader blends the far wall (darker) under each pixel, as a draw of the back faces would: on a round bottle the far wall's normal is the front normal mirrored about the view direction (`2(n.v)v - n`). A disc closes the mouth a little down the neck (`MOUTH`), and the shader draws it as the inside only; it is a lathe of its own, because in the same lathe it bent the lip normals and the lip caught a highlight. A third draw of the back faces cost a draw call and its fill for the same look. The glass is clear at the middle and thick at the edges (`GLASS.alpha`, from N.V). The outline is drawn round the back faces, so it would show through the glass: the glass writes `GLASS.stencil` (8, above the ibis's 1 to 5) and the outline draws only where the stencil is not 8. The ibis reads its stencil in the opaque pass, before any glass. The glass casts a solid shadow. The toon and outline shaders in `props.js` take the instance matrix (`MODEL`).
- Keep a hinge limit within ±π (as in Jolt), so a flap opens to 2.6 at most, not flat on the side.
- `addBox()` keeps the convex radius below half the thinnest half size: the default (0.05) is more than the half thickness of a 0.03 flap.
- Slam dust: `Slam.update()` (for the bin and the boxes) keeps the fastest spin of a fall with its velocities (`peak`). When the spin drops below `BIN.slam.keep` of it, the bin sends a `slam` event with points on its low edges, each with the speed of the hit there (v + w x r). Only the fast points (the top end) make dust, in `IbisEffects.burst()`. A check of two frames in a row missed hits that fell in one long frame. Edges within `BIN.slam.low` of the lowest corner count, as the open lid holds the top end up a little.
- Bird dust starts at `controller.floor`, so a landing on the bin puts its dust on the bin.
- Bin wheels (`BIN.wheel`) sit outside the side walls at the back bottom corners, on an axle, with a hub: wheels set into the back face read as half circles. They are only in the mesh: the solid is the tub. A square blue sticker, a little skewed, is on the -x side (`BIN.sticker`), the side the default camera sees.
- The bin and the boxes are hollow, open at the top (under the lid or the flaps). Physics: `addHollowBox()`, one compound body of a floor and four walls (`BIN.wall`, `BIN.floor`, `CARTON.wall`), so a ball can fall in; its centre of mass is set as `com` from the middle of the box, as `addBox()`, so the tip behaviour stays the same. Mesh: `openBox()` in `props.js` gives the outside with the rim, and the inside (walls and floor) in a darker colour (`inside`, `cardInside`), with no outline. Cost: about 30 triangles for each prop, and five boxes in the physics in place of one. The bird: a cap over the top keeps it out of an upright prop, and it can duck into a fallen one (caps and duck in `physics.md`).
- A scaled sphere does not collide as an ellipsoid: the ball is a convex hull of points on the ellipsoid.
- Flies (`flies.js`): `flies: N` (or `[min, max]`, from a hash of the prop index) on a prop in the level data puts N flies round the middle of its top (the general waste bins in the street, not the recycling). `Level.populate()` makes one `Flies` for all of them. Each fly darts from point to point over the lid (`FLIES.lid`) and sometimes lands (`FLIES.sit`): on the lid of an upright bin (only when `perch()` gives a top), and on a bin that is down, on the side that faces up or on the ground beside it (not in front of the open end: the open lid lies there and hid them). A fly on the bin keeps its spot in bin space and takes off when its side turns away from up. In flight, `outside()` keeps each fly and each new point out of the box of the tub and the lid: a fly that would go in moves out through the nearest side and loses its velocity into that side, so it slides round the bin. The box is the solid tub, so a fly does not go into an open bin. A fly that sits shows only its body: folded wings below it looked like a shadow. A bird within `FLIES.scare.radius` of the top, or a bin that moves faster than `scare.bump`, scatters them up and away from the bird. Each fly comes back after its own calm time (`scare.back`), so they return one by one. Size: `FLIES.size` is about five times a real fly; at true size they read as dust. A sitting fly is lifted by its body radius, or it sinks into the lid. Past `FLIES.far` from the camera a swarm does no work and does not draw. Cost: about 0 (10 quads, one draw, unlit, `alphaToCoverage` as the tufts).
- Gate (`gate.js`, `Gate`): a swing gate in a fence: `{ kind: 'gate', at, heading, width, hinge }` in an area's props. `at` is the middle of the gap, on a fence run (within `GATE_ON` of it, `fence.js`); `width` is post centre to post centre. The fence cuts the gap, and its posts at the gap ends are the gate posts. The gate is only the leaf: a dynamic box (`GATE.mass` 3, heavy layer) on a hinge to a static body inside the hinge post, about y, free to `GATE.swing` (90 degrees) each way. The hinge spring (`GATE.spring`: 0.6 Hz, damping 0.45) takes it back to shut, past shut once and back. The bird pushes it as a prop, so a walk opens it about 70 degrees and it follows the bird through. `GATE.clear` keeps the leaf off the posts as it turns. The bird's push is capped at its own speed, so a run also opens it about 70 degrees. A rusty hinge creak (event `creak`, `sound.creak()`) as it opens: one each `GATE.creak.step` (about 26 degrees) of turn, faster turn louder, so a walk through gives two. None as the spring takes it back. A clang at the end of the swing was cut: the gate never got there. The mesh: four rounded rails, a brace from the bottom at the hinge to the top at the latch, the fence wire (`wireMaterial()`), rusty knuckles and a latch. Rust (`RUST`): a band round each knuckle and the rail ends near it, and a drip (wide, then narrow) down the stile. Thin boxes `STAIN` proud of the paint, no outline. The shade tones are light: a dark shade read as a slot in the stile. The tufts are cleared in its swing.
- Sprinkler (`sprinkler.js`, `Sprinkler`): an oscillating lawn sprinkler in the park, on a hose from a tap inside the gate (`tap` in the level data). The tap stands on its own pipe. Light layer, 0.9 kg (`SPRINKLER`): the feet and the head knock it about. Its sizes are made at scale 1 and multiplied by `scale` (1.5: at 1 it was too small to read next to the bird); the tap by `tap.scale`, and its handle by `tap.handle`, larger again, so it reads as something to use. Prop updates get `(ibises, dt, camera)`, and the level gives each prop `height(x, z)` (the ground) in its data.
  - Tap: chrome, with a cross head on top: four spokes with round knobs. A peck turns it: the handle is a switch (`Physics.addSwitch()`, a box `tap.reach` round it from the tap head to over the handle, not a body), so the peck highlight outlines it (`hull(id)`) and the peck's `grab` turns it in place of a pick up, with a squeak (`turn` event). A cross looks the same after a quarter turn, so it turns a full turn: anticlockwise to open (to 0), clockwise to shut (to -2 pi), easing at `tap.turn`. The water and the hiss show its state. The bird reaches it from up to about 1.75 from the tap. The water pressure follows the tap (`tap.rise` each second): the drop rate, and the speed down to `tap.low` of full. It starts open. Day and night do not change it: at night, a tap that the player opened and that did nothing looked broken.
  - Spray: while the tap is open, and only within `far` of the camera. The bar sweeps the spray about its length (`bar.sweep`, `bar.period`); each drop starts along the bar, out in a fan (`spray.fan`, `scatter`), at `spray.speed`. The spray comes from the body's pose, so a kicked sprinkler sprays where it points. On its side, half of each sweep went into the ground and the spray looked broken: `aim()` turns the middle of the sweep, smoothly (`bar.follow`), just enough that it is `bar.low` radians above level. At 1.0 the spray ran on 74% of the frames on its side; at 1.25, 87%. Upright it does not turn.
  - Upside down: the solid is a hull that narrows to a ridge along the bar (`ridge`), so it can't rest upside down: it rolls onto a side. If it balances on the ridge for `flip.still` seconds, a push up on one side (`flip.push`) rolls it over. No sound while upside down.
  - Hose: `hose.slack` times the first distance to the tap. Past that, a spring on the inlet pulls it back (`hose.pull`, `damp`). A hard kick goes about 1.6 past the length, then comes back. The hose and the tap are one mesh with the shared prop materials. The tube's vertices are made once and moved in place when the sprinkler moves, with only their range uploaded: no garbage. The tube's outline normal is its normal. No shadow: it lies on the ground.
  - Soak: a drop inside a bird's column (`soak.reach`, `soak.top`) gives `{ type: 'soak', ibis, wet, wash }`. `Level` raises the wet target and lowers the mud target. Standing in the sweep: about 0.7 wet in 6 s; the bird is hit only in its half of the sweep.
  - Drops (`Drops`): a ring buffer of `spray.count` streaks, one instanced draw, alphaToCoverage, unlit. A flight is a parabola, so the vertex shader places a drop from its start, velocity and start time (`aStart`, `aVel`), and hides it after its life. Only new drops upload (`addUpdateRange()`), not all drops each frame. The landing time and place are found once when a drop starts (`flight()`: again at the landing place, for its ground height). A drop that lands on higher ground than it starts from is still a full flight: an early version ended it at once, and on its side (nozzle 0.06 up, footpath 0.1) almost no drop showed. Hit tests run only while a bird is within the spray's reach. The mesh hides when no drop is in the air. A drop's quad: x is to the right of its motion, or it faces away and is culled.
  - Wet patch (`WetGround`): a grid round the tap (`wet.cell`, out to the hose length and `wet.margin`), as a one byte texture (`unpackAlignment` 1: the rows are not a multiple of 4 bytes, and the upload failed). Each landing wets its cell and half as much round it (`wet.add`); one cell alone gave specks. The wet cells (a set, not the whole grid) dry by `wet.dry` each second, and the texture uploads every `wet.upload` seconds when a cell changed. The ground shader darkens with it (`GROUND_WET`, `environment.md`). About 40 s to a full patch; about 4 minutes to dry.
  - Cost: 7 draws (body, hose and tap, cross head: toon and outline each; drops), plus the body's and the cross head's shadows. The update is under the browser timer's 0.1 ms. GPU: a frame timer query swung from 2.7 to 6.8 ms for the same build, so measure parts (`tools.md`). The drops timed the same with no upload and at zero size: their fill is below what the timer shows. The wet patch on and off in the ground draw was within the noise (±0.3 ms).
- Demo: props are on in free play. `props=0` turns them off, `physics=1` shows wireframes of the colliders (the debug renderer loads only then). `window.demo` has `level`, `physics`, `props`, `fx`, `camera` and `controls` for the console and the tools.
