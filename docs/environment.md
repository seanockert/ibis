# Environment

Street, fence, tufts, sky, backdrop, grade and the free play camera (`src/world/`, `src/levels/street.js`, `src/app/`).

- Scale: the bird is 2 units, so 1 unit is about 35 cm. Keep the street in rough proportion, a little smaller than real, as in a cartoon: footpath 3, nature strip 6, road 14, bin 3 tall.
- Layout: strips along x (`STRIPS` in `street.js`). The road is on the -z side, so the default camera looks across the nature strip and the road. The bird starts on the nature strip.
- The ground is one mesh that follows the camera along x: `PROFILE` across z, straight along x. The road is `KERB.height` (0.2) lower than the rest, with a rolled kerb on each side: a flat gutter, then an S curve up to the top (less than `GROUND.stepUp`, so the bird walks up it). `PROFILE` is the one source of the heights: the mesh, the physics ground (`new Physics({ ground: PROFILE })`), the footprints (`fx.height`) and the tap to walk (`streetHeight()`). Its shader draws the strips in world space, with the toon band (`toonBand()`, so a slope that turns from the sun gets the shade) and a cool form tone where N.L is below `FORM`. A dark line on the kerb lip and a contact shade in the gutter make the drop read from the top side, where the roll is out of view.
- Footpaths: `PATH.height` (0.1) above the grass, with a round edge (an S curve `PATH.edge` wide) on each long side, in `PROFILE`: real geometry, as the kerb, because the bird steps on it and the props rest on it (about 16 triangles). The slab ends at the joints have a bevel in the shader only (`PATH.bevel`, `PATH.slope`): it tilts the normal toward the joint, so the end away from the sun takes the cool form tone. The round side of a slab that faces away from the sun is darker concrete (`pathSide`), not the lavender shade and the form tone: that read as a purple band. A path slope that faces the sun on the ground plane gets a light lip (7%), and the grass has a cool contact shade along each path edge (`PATH.contact`). Things that stand on a footpath take `streetHeight()`: the props (through `ground.height` of the level, see `levels.md`), the joint tufts (`aGround`) and the footprints. A normal map or parallax mapping would cost more and add nothing at this size and view; keep real geometry for heights that play touches, and shading for the rest.
- `line(u, width)` in the ground shader takes its pixel width from `fwidth(u)`. For a u that wraps (the joints `jx`, the road dashes) pass `fwidth(p.x)`: at the wrap `fwidth(u)` jumps and draws a faint line in the middle of each slab.
- Ground depth: the ground writes its depth `DEPTH_BIAS` (0.2) behind it along the view ray, so the outlines round the feet still show and a kerb hides what is below it on the road. Less than 0.12 cuts the outline under the toes. The bias is in the vertex shader, on a grid mesh (`GRID`: 3 unit cells near the camera and the play area, larger away from them, about 7k triangles): the depth is straight between the vertices, and the error is about (cell / distance)² / 4 of the bias. Do not go back to `gl_FragDepth`: a depth write in the fragment shader turns off the early depth test, so every ground pixel ran the full shader, also under the props (0.9 ms at ratio 1.5). The rows go from the lowest to the highest.
- Draw order: the solid scenery and props draw before the ground (`BEFORE_GROUND` in `level.js`, renderOrder -2; the ground is -1), so the depth test skips the ground pixels under them (about 0.7 ms). They all write depth, so no pixel changes (a pixel diff showed at most 1 pixel). The puddle (-0.75), the footprints (-0.5) and the birds (0) draw after the ground: the puddle and the prints write no depth. A new opaque object that writes no depth and lies on the ground needs a renderOrder between -1 and 0.
- Road: dark slate (`COLOURS.road` in `street.js`), so the light kerbs and the grass stand out.
- Grass (`street.js`): the sunny and lush patches have hard toon edges, one pixel wide. `GRASS` (`grassPatches()`, `grassColour()`) is shared by the ground and the tufts, so a tuft root has the colour of the ground under it. `#define FRAGMENT` before the chunk in a fragment shader turns on the pixel-wide edge (the vertex shader has no `fwidth`).
- Painted marks (`MARKS` in `street.js`): small tufts drawn flat in the ground shader in some cells of a grid, stretched so they look upright to the camera. `BLADES` (`tuft()`) gives the same shape as the 3D tufts. Each pixel tests 2 by 2 cells, so a mark must stay inside half a cell (the stretch is clamped at a low view). They are off past the patch fade (`near`).
- Pebbles (`PEBBLES` in `street.js`): round mounds in the ground shader near the strip edges (more nearer an edge, from the root's `edgeDistance()`), upright to the camera as the marks: light top, cool underside, small contact shadow. Not on the kerbs and the road: pale dots there read as road marks. Flat ovals on the ground were thin slivers at the play camera.
- Mulch (`MULCH` in `street.js`): patches of dirt and bark through the grass round the foot of each tree, in the grass branch of the ground shader. The level gives the beds (`BEDS` in `src/levels/street.js`, from `PLANTED`: a gum 2.6, a bottlebrush 2.0; shrubs have none) to `Street` and `Tufts`. Up to `MULCH.max` (8) beds in a uniform. A patch noise less a fall off from the trunk: full near it, small patches out to `reach` of the radius. Hard toon edge, the colour `tint` (0.6) of the mulch over the grass under it, so it takes the grass patches and blends, and a few bark chips near the camera. A solid brown bed with a dark rim and a plum shade read as too dark and muddy: keep it a tint, and the shade near the grass shade. Tufts grow in the outer patches, not inside `clear` of the radius (`inBed()`). The footprints on mulch are still the grass prints.
- Clover and bees (`clover.js`, `bees.js`, shared insect code in `insects.js`): add `{ at, radius, bees }` to `CLOVER` in `src/levels/street.js`. The same list clears tufts (`clear` in `tufts.js`).
  - Clover: base dome, outlined clump domes, white balls on stems (`toonParts()`). Clumps 0.22 to 0.32 tall. Not good: small flat leaves (busy), cards turned to the camera (flat from above), one bumpy cushion (bumps never took the shade band).
  - Bees: flower to flower, drift, hop to another patch, keep off flowers near a bird (`shy`), scatter from a bird (`scatter`). Day only (`LIGHT.night` up to `BEES.night`). Fade in nearer than `near`, out past `far`.
  - Popping, fixed: a bee facing left is a mirrored quad, so the material must be `DoubleSide`. Also: `pull` (grass hid low bees), the near and far gap (far bees blinked), `flip` only on a clear sideways flight.
  - The bird's feet go into the clover: the ground height does not include it. Cost: about 0.1 ms GPU.
- Cracks (`CRACKS` in `street.js`): in some footpath slabs, a cartoon zigzag of three straight strokes from one side. Only footpath pixels run the code. Small tufts grow in some footpath joints (`TUFTS.joints`).
- Ground noise: `noise2()` (`src/world/noise.js`) reads a tiling texture of value noise, in place of `noise3()` (8 sin hashes): the grass patches (and the tuft roots), the mulch and the road repairs. Each texel holds the 4 lattice values round its cell and the shader blends them. With the hardware linear filter (one read for each noise, the smooth step in the coordinate), the 8-bit filter weights made jagged steps on the patch toon edges. The patches are a different random field from the old noise, with the same statistics.
- Ground cost: the grass patches and the marks run only near a grass strip (`onGrass`, with a margin wide enough that the branch edge has no grass weight), the cracks only on the footpaths, the pebbles only near an edge and not on the kerbs and the road.
- `Upright` in the ground shader: the frame for the marks and the pebbles, made once in `main()`, outside any branch, because `fwidth()` in a branch is undefined in GLSL.
- Warm and cool: the lit ground colours are warm (yellow-green, peach), the shade colours cool (teal, lavender), far apart on purpose. `GRADE` adds to it: a cool lavender lift in the dark parts, a warm gain in the light parts.
- Tufts (`tufts.js`): one family, from thin grass (light, warm) to a few wide leaves (darker, cooler), from `aLeafy`. Each tuft is one flat green (a cooler one in the sun's shadow). A two-tone split per leaf looked too busy, and a light normal (ball or cylinder) split the tufts with a flat line across: do not go back to them. The root colour comes from `grassPatches()` in the vertex shader, once for each tuft. Placement by rules in `place()`: clusters (one big tuft, small ones round it, one kind) where a value noise is high, runs along the strip edges with gaps, and long thin grass round the foot of each fence post (`TUFTS.posts`, from `fencePosts()` in `fence.js`; not on the footpath), all round each lamp post (`TUFTS.poles`, the level passes `poles`), and patches of long grass where a second noise is high (`TUFTS.long`: a jittered grid, narrower tufts (`wide`), knee high at the patch edge and as high as the bird's head in the middle, so the bird can hide). Dry tufts: grey straw (`DRY`), in patches of a third noise and a few anywhere (`TUFTS.dry`, `dryness()`). Dryness comes from the place, not from the `hash()` sequence, so it moves no tuft. None near a tree trunk (`inBed()`). New rules go after the old ones in `place()`: each tuft takes values from one `hash()` sequence, so a rule put first moves all the tufts after it. Wind: waves in world space and a small flutter, a shear of the quad top (`TUFTS.wind`). The birds push tufts aside (`TUFTS.push`): each tuft near a bird has a spring on the CPU (`push()`), pulled away from the bird and along its move, so it parts as the bird walks through and wobbles back after. The spring's bend goes to the shader in `aBend`. Only tufts near a bird or still moving are in `active`; a grid of 1 unit cells finds them. A tuft under the bird swings over to the other side as the bird walks over it. A tall tuft springs slower (`push.tall`). `rustle` (amount, place, dryness of the moving grass) goes to `sound.rustle()` from `Level.updateView()`. `alphaToCoverage` gives soft edges with the multisampled target of `grade.js`, with no sorting. Far tufts shrink to nothing at `TUFTS.fade`. Props: each prop has `solids()` (`{ body, half, round }`); the demo passes them to `update()`, and a tuft is no taller than the distance from its root to the nearest solid (`MAX_SOLIDS`), so no grass goes through a box. A new prop needs `solids()`. About 4300 tufts (3000 of them in the long grass), one draw call. Each card is a hull round the blades (`HULL`, 12 vertices), not a quad: the blades fill only about half of the quad, and the shader runs on each pixel of the card. The hull came from a measure of `tuft()` over many seeds; a change to `BLADES` needs a new measure (a pixel diff against full width cards cut at the same heights shows a clipped blade). Tufts within `TUFTS.near` of the camera shrink to nothing, so a tall tuft never fills the screen. Cost (GPU, one draw, ratio 1.5, Radeon Pro 570): about 0.2 ms at the start, about 0.4 ms with the camera at the head high grass (0.1 ms before the long grass). The hull took off about 28% in the long grass. Tried with no gain: one tap of the shadow maps in place of `sunShadow()`, an integer hash in `bladeRnd()`, 3 blades at most for far tufts, and the tufts sorted near to far.
- The sky is a sphere that follows the camera, drawn after the opaque scene (`renderOrder 2`, after the bird outlines at 1 and before the anger lines at 3, which write no depth), on the far plane with `LessEqualDepth`: the depth test skips the pixels that the ground and the props cover. That saved about 0.35 ms in free play. An opaque object that writes no depth and has nothing behind it would be painted over by the sky: give it a `renderOrder` above 2. Clouds are circles in azimuth and elevation (`CLOUDS`, made with `hash()`). The far hills and trees are bands in the sky shader, with soft edges as a fake depth of field.
- Far ground fades to `HAZE` (pale sage), and the sky fills below the trees with the same colour, so the ground meets the horizon band. A blue haze read as a lake.
- Colours: picked from the concept art with a colour picker. Warm in the sun (yellow-green grass, peach footpath), cool in the shade (teal grass, lavender footpath). Tune against the concept, not by eye alone.
- Backdrop: the horizon must be close, or the street feels empty. Two rings of upright quads along rounded boxes (`RINGS`): a near green one just past the yards, and a taller, hazier one behind it. The shader cuts a canopy line (slow crowns plus round clumps) and shades the clumps. Transparent, soft edges, no depth write. The quads stop at the highest canopy, so the empty part costs nothing.
- Clouds: few (`CLOUDS.count` 8) and low, so the hills and the trees cover their feet. They drift with the wind (`speed`, radians a second; `uTime` from `Sky.update(camera, dt)`, so the fixed views stay at time 0), bob a little, and the bumps swell and shrink (`morph`). Each has its own stretch, dome size and, for some, a top bump. A cheap box test skips the cloud far from a pixel. About 0.1 ms more than static clouds in a view of mostly sky. Each is a wide flat ellipse base, a dome off centre and a small bump on each side, white with a pale cool underside and a faint shade on the dome away from the sun. Keep them simple: puffy clouds of many circles looked busy. Cloud white stays a little below 1.0.
- Grade (`grade.js`): the target is 8-bit sRGB (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`, 4x MSAA): the hardware encodes on write and decodes on read, so the shaders see linear colour, with even steps in the dark. With `env` the canvas has no MSAA, depth or stencil (`src/app/stage.js`) and the quad does not clear it: it only gets the full-screen quad. The depth and stencil buffers of the canvas and their clear cost about 0.9 ms at ratio 1.5 on the iMac's Radeon Pro 570. Lifts dark parts toward lavender, warms light parts (not blue ones, or the sky goes teal), adds a little saturation and a warm vignette. It runs only with `env`, so the fixed views are unchanged. About 1 ms; the sky is about 1 ms more. The three.js `EffectComposer` with `UnrealBloomPass` cost about 5 ms: do not use it for a faint glow.
- Fixed views keep the plain background (`env=0`), so they still match `sdf.html`. `env=1` turns the street on for screenshots.
- Fence (`fence.js`, with `env`): the visible fence is `FENCE.out` (0.6) outside `PLAY`, on the yard grass 1.1 past the footpath (so the post tufts grow from grass, not the pavement), so a ball stops at the posts. Posts and rails: `toonMaterial()` and `outlineMaterial()` on instanced meshes (posts and rails cast shadows). Wire: one mesh of quads, a diamond pattern with `alphaToCoverage`, blended to a flat tint where a diamond is a few pixels (no shimmer); it does not cast a shadow (the shadow pass ignores the alpha). There is no fence at the two ends of the play area (a fence across the footpaths and the road read badly): the bounds stop the bird there, and the physics fence stops the props. With physics, each run is also a thin static wall on the `terrain` layer (as high as the posts), so heavy props (the bin, the boxes) can't go through. Size: posts 3.2 tall (a real 1.2 m fence is 3.4 units), above the flutter height (2.0). `perches()`: the post tops and the middle of each rail top, where the flying miners land. The post tops are the highest perches, so the miners guard from them. The near fence is under the default free play camera, so the street puts the miners on the far fence.
- Demo camera: in free play, `Follow` (`src/app/follow.js`) moves the camera and its orbit centre together toward a point ahead of the bird (`CAMERA`): a short look-ahead along the velocity that builds and turns slowly (`lead`, `leadFreq`), critically damped springs for the follow (`follow`), a soft dead zone (`slack`), a stiffer follow when the bird is far from the centre (`catchUp`), and up: the floor in full, part of a jump (`rise`) with a lead on the way up (`riseLead`). Orbit with a drag still works.
- Camera swing: as the bird moves, the view turns behind it (`swing`). The rate is the speed times the sine of the angle from behind, as in Mario 64: a sideways move makes a wide arc, and a move straight at the camera does not turn it. A drag that turns the view stops the swing for `resume` seconds. A tap or a zoom does not. A short tap on the middle of the joystick (`STICK_TAP`, `TAP_MS` in `input.js`) calls `Follow.recentre()`: the view turns behind the bird and back to the start pitch (`recentre`, a half-life).
- Camera glance: `Follow.glance(point)` turns the view part of the way toward a point out of view and moves the orbit centre a little toward it, holds, and comes back (`CAMERA.glance`). The miners sit higher than the follow camera looks, so the glance also tilts the view up: the camera orbits lower until the point is at `top` of the half height of the view above the middle (a search by halves, `tiltTo()`), at most `maxTilt` and not below the orbit floor. `main.js` gives the miner's head, and `top` leaves room for the mark over it. A point near the middle of the view gets no glance. A glance asked for while one plays waits, and starts when that one ends (only the last one waits). The turn and the move go on top of the follow and the swing: each frame takes off the last turn and tilt, does the swing, then turns and tilts again. `main.js` calls it for each `alert` event of `Sentry`, so the player sees a miner that goes on alert out of view.
- Camera shake: `Follow.shake()` (a snap that hurts a miner, in `main.js`) jolts the camera and the orbit centre together for `CAMERA.shake.time`, falling to nothing. Each update takes off the last jolt first, so the follow springs never see it.
- Move input (`Input.apply()`): camera relative, from the view of each frame, as in Mario 64. With the swing, forward and to the side makes a continuous curve, and only to the side runs in a circle round the camera. Do not lock the input to the view of when the key went down: then forward and to the side turns 45 degrees and stops, which does not feel like a 3D platformer.
- Zoom: in free play the orbit can't go farther than `CAM_MAX_PLAY` (18, in `stage.js`) from its centre. The start distance is 12.5.
- See-through (`SEE`, `seeOn`, `SEE_THROUGH` in `props.js`): the toon and outline shaders of the props and the fence posts and rails, and the fence wire, fade only where the camera is in or near the object. Two parts: the shader fades what is within `SEE.near` of the camera (the wire, and a face the near plane would cut), and a whole object fades while the camera is within `SEE.margin` of it. A whole object fades, not a circle round the camera: a dithered circle that slid along a post looked bad. `SEE.margin` is not less than `SEE.near`, so no part of an object fades on its own. A prop: `seeInside()` (from `Level.updateView()`) swaps it to faded twins of the shared materials (`FADED`) near one of its `solids()` boxes. A bin needs this: from inside, its walls are too far for the first part. A mesh of many objects (the plants, the fence posts, the fence rails) cannot swap materials: each vertex or instance has the id of its object (`aSeeId`, with `SEE_ID`), and the mesh's `seeIds()` uniform holds the ids (up to 4) that fade, set each frame by `update()`. The tests: a capsule round each post and rail; a capsule round each branch and a sphere round each leaf ball of a plant, larger by as much as the wind moves them (`swayReach()`). Both cost about 2 microseconds a frame. Without the plant fade, the camera in a trunk, a crown or a shrub showed the back faces of the outline as a dark fill. Do not fade what is between the camera and the player: a see-through hole round the bird was not wanted. `main.js` turns it on in free play; in the other views it is off, so they do not change. The toon parts use a 4 by 4 Bayer dither (discard), so they stay opaque and need no sort. The outline is off in the whole fade: a dithered outline showed through the gaps of the part. The wire multiplies its alphaToCoverage cover. The stubby glass, the tufts and the ground do not fade. A new prop material must call `seeThrough()` to fade.
- With a 4 s lead and a loose follow, the bird ran off a portrait phone screen (400x800) at a run: the lead went to the side of a narrow view. Check the follow at a portrait size. Fixed views (`t`, `yaw`) do not follow. The orbit can't take the camera below `CAM_MIN_Y` (`Stage.keepAbove()`) (`maxPolarAngle` is set each frame from the distance).
- Plants (`plants.js`, `PLANTED` in `src/levels/street.js`): gums, bottlebrushes and shrubs in the far yard, between the fence (z -27.1) and the backdrop (z -42), and one bottlebrush on the near nature strip at x 20, down the street from the start (a trunk solid, and a bare perch branch toward the start with a miner on it), and three shrubs on the same strip at x 40 to 45 (each with a solid core). Each grows from its seed (its place in the list, or `seed`), so no two are the same: the height, the lean, the fork, the limbs and side branches, the clumps and the flowers. The seed also turns the plant about y. A tree: a tapered tube trunk (`tube()`, 7 sides, a flare at the foot) to the fork, limbs out and curving up, a leaf clump at each branch end. A shrub: one clump, its shell down to the ground and its low balls `SINK` (0.4) of their radius in the ground: a shrub with its lowest ball just on the ground looked as if it floated. A clump: one big ball in the middle and balls on a shell round it (more on top, smaller below).
- Leaf shading (`leafMaterial()`, `LEAF`): two tones (shade, lit), as the concept art. The toon band is on a normal between the ball normal and the clump's ellipsoid normal (`aCanopy`), so each clump reads as one shape. Round leaf lobes (`lobe()`: one minus the distance to the nearest point of a jittered 3D grid, on `aLobe`) push N.L up and down, so the light edge is a chain of round lobes. `aLobe` is the rest position in clump space, `LEAF.lobes` lobes across a clump: a gum clump and a bottlebrush clump get lobes of the same count, not the same size. Value noise on N.L gave thin streaks, not lobes. The sun is almost overhead, so with the real sun the light edge was a flat band across each clump: the leaf light has the sun's ground part `LEAF.side` larger. The leaves do not take the shadow map: the sway and the coarse far map gave streaks and soft bands on the crowns. A sunny top tone and a darker underside were too busy. The colours are from the concept with a colour sampler (k-means of the green pixels, two groups): render a crown and check them with the same sampler, as the grade changes them. The lobes cost under 0.02 ms. The leaf outline (`LINE_BACK`, `back` in `outlineMaterial()`) takes the depth of a point 4 units farther from the camera, so it shows only against what is far behind the crown: no lines between the balls of a crown, only round its outside, as the concept.
- Plant sizes: a gum about 25 units (9 m), a bottlebrush about 11 (4 m), a shrub about the bird's height. A trunk is wider than the bird (about 1 unit): a gum trunk is 1.6 units at the foot, a bottlebrush trunk 1.1. At half these sizes the trees looked like toys next to the bird. Keep the limbs long and the clumps small on a gum: big clumps on short limbs read as a lollipop. A gum has an open crown (`open()`): a leaf clump at each branch end, with sky between. A bottlebrush has a dome crown (`dome()`, `crown`): 16 to 20 clumps on the shell of a round crown, in a spiral from the top, and only some (`under`) below its middle, so it reads as one full, round shape and the branches show through the gaps under it, as the concept and real trees. Its limbs fork low (`fork` 0.12 to 0.17 of its height), each splits in two (a Y) into the crown, and a twig goes from the nearest branch to each clump. Clumps only at branch ends, small to show the branches, read as tufts on sticks; one long straight limb to the crown read as a pole. Points on the leaf balls (the corners pushed out) read as low-poly artifacts, not leaves: at 80 triangles a ball, leaf shapes must come from the shading or from leaf cards, not from the mesh. The flowers are brushes (`brush()`): a tube with a star section of 12 sides (ridges, so the outline reads as bristles), near straight, closed to a point at each end. Capsules read as red crystals; a round profile read as tulips. Gum bark shade is a warm grey, not the lavender of the posts: lavender read as a purple trunk.
- Plant cost: the leaf balls are icosphere detail 1 (80 triangles; detail 2 doubled the triangles of the frame for a small gain). A leaf triangle with its corners inside another ball (`HIDDEN`) is left out: about half of them. The plants are in chunks `CHUNK` (32) wide along x, four draws each (bark and flowers, their outline, leaves, the leaf outline), so the camera and the shadow pass skip the chunks out of their view. One merged mesh for all plants was always in view (its bounding sphere was the whole street). The street's 15 plants: 130k triangles in all meshes, 15 draws; 0.15 to 0.3 ms GPU in free play at 1200x800, with the shadow (was 0.4 ms with one mesh and all triangles at half the size).
- Far shadow (`farShadow.js`): the sun's map covers only 50 by 50 round the camera, so a tree farther away lost its shadow and got it back as the camera came near. The plants (leaves and bark, `FAR_LAYER`) also draw once into a static map over all of them (2048, about 0.04 units a texel), in the wind of time 0. `sunShadow()` fades from the sun's map to this map near the edge of the sun's map, so a plant shadow stays at any distance. It costs nothing each frame, except the 4 taps (under 0.05 ms). Props and the fence are not in it: they move, or are small. It is on with `env` only (`FAR_SHADOW.on`); the fixed views of the bird do not change. The bias is a move of the receiver toward the sun (in the matrix) and along its normal, in units, so it does not change with the depth range.
- Tall trees: the sun of the free play shadow is `SHADOW.sun` (45) from the area's centre. At 20, the crown of a 25 unit gum was behind the sun's near plane and had no shadow. The depth bias (`SHADOW.bias`) is for that range.
- LOD: not done. The draws are a few for each chunk, not for each plant, so the count of plants changes only the triangles. The far shadow is the one LOD: far plants cost no shadow pass. A far version of a chunk (fewer, larger balls, no flowers) would pop at the switch; to hide it, cross-fade the two with the Bayer dither of `SEE_THROUGH`. Billboards of the far trees (impostors) are the next step after that. Culling the crown tops (the camera is low) breaks when the camera glances up at a miner. Add a far chunk only with a profile that shows a need: at 0.3 ms for all plants, there is none.
- Wind (`wind.js`): the plants sway along `WIND.dir` (as the tufts): a steady lean, a slow rock and gusts that cross the street in waves. The crowns also flutter (`WIND.flutter`): a small quick wobble in x, y and z, with a phase from the position, so each clump moves out of step with the next, more in a gust. The sway alone moved the whole tree as one, and too slowly to see; at a `sway` of 0.25 a bottlebrush crown moved about 0.15 units. The flutter weight is the square root of `aSway`, at most 1, so a shrub top flutters a little. The position field is smooth, so the limbs and the clumps stay joined. `aSway` on each vertex: 0 at the foot, (height / `WIND.height`)^1.5 above it, so a gum top moves most and a shrub hardly moves. The same `sway()` in the leaf shader, the toon and outline materials (`wind: true` in `toonMaterial()`, `outlineMaterial()` and `toonParts()`; props without it do not change) and the shadow (`windDepthMaterial()`, `customDepthMaterial`), so the outlines and the shadows move with the leaves. The leaf noise uses the rest position, or the light edge crawls. `Plants.update()` steps `windTime`.
- A plant in the play area: a plant in `PLAY` gets a solid (a prism of 8 sides round the foot) on the `terrain` layer. The bird stops at it and slides round. A tree: its trunk, `SOLID` wider, to the fork. A shrub: a core, as the branches in it (`CORE`: 0.7 of its smaller half width, 0.7 of its height). The bird stops at the edge, about 1.1 to 1.4 units from the middle, with only the front of the body and the head in the leaves. At 0.45, the bird went in to about 1 unit, half the body in the leaves. The branches of a tree have no solid. A crown over the play area hides the bird when it walks behind it (the see-through fade is only at the camera): keep street trees few and to the side. The tufts do not know the trunk (`MAX_SOLIDS` is already full of props).
- Plan for later phases: midground fences, letterbox, gobo noise for dappled shade, more plants from the concept (jacaranda, palm, citrus, lomandra, hedge); a backdrop of houses drawn to a half-size render target and blurred (depth of field); footprint and dust style for each surface.
- Day cycle (`DayCycle` in `lighting.js`): the time button or N steps day, dusk, night, morning (`CYCLE.time` seconds each); `time=dusk` (or `night=1`) for screenshots. `CYCLE` holds the keys of each period: `LIGHT.night`, lamp `power`, the key light (the shared sun direction: high sun, low west sun, moon, low east sun; slerped), and the drawn sun and moon (`LIGHT.sunOrb`, `moonOrb`). The drawn ones only rise and set straight up and down at their own azimuth (below the tree line at `orb.below`); morning to day, the sun goes up and over. Dusk and morning add a toon colour cast (`uTint`) and sky colours (`DUSK_COLOURS`, `MORNING_COLOURS` in `sky.js`) over the day to night blend. Stars only past half night. The far map redraws every 6th frame of a step.
- Night colour: every toon shader makes its colour with the band `lampBand(band, lamp)` and then calls `night()` (or `toonLit()` for both): a moon multiply (`NIGHT_MOON`), warm in a pool (`NIGHT_LAMP`). A pool lifts a sun or moon shadow by `LAMP_FILL` (0.5) only. At 1, the shadows stopped with a hard edge at the pool rim. With `uNight` 0 both return at once, so the day is unchanged (pixel diff).
- Lamps: up to `MAX_LAMPS` (4) slots, the nearest lit lamps within `LAMP.near` of the camera. Pools do not overlap, so a pixel takes the one lamp it is under: one shadow lookup in one atlas (2 by 2 tiles, `MeshDepthMaterial` over the meshes on `CASTER_LAYER`, the ones with `castShadow`). One tile redraws a frame: the nearest lamp on even frames, the others in turn. Do not use three.js `SpotLight`s: four spot shadows in every toon shader cost about 1.2 ms even when off, from the code, not the work. Keep lamps 30 or more apart. A lamp in `PLAY` has a solid.
- Wet ground (`GROUND_WET` in `street.js`): the sprinkler's wet grid (`props.md`) as a texture and its area. The ground shader samples it inside the area only and multiplies the colour by `WET.tint` past a wetness edge (`WET.edge`), with noise (`WET.noise`, `WET.scale`) so the edge is blotchy: darker grass, grey concrete. Before the night tint, so the lamps still light it.
- Frame cost (GPU, 1200x800, play start, `npm run gpu`, Radeon Pro 570): at ratio 1.5, day 6.9 ms, dusk and night 7.6 ms; lite (ratio 1.25) 5.3 ms. Before the ground and grade work of October 2026: 9.4, 10.2, 10.2 and 6.9. Of the day frame: the ground about 1.9 ms, the 4x MSAA about 1.3 ms, the birds 0.9 ms, the grade quad, the tufts 0.3 ms. Night adds about 0.7 ms, from the lamp and night code in the shaders, not the lamp shadow redraws (about 0). The sun's map redraws every 2nd frame (`SHADOW.every`: about 0.6 ms on those frames; its matrix only moves when it redraws). Free play lowers the pixel ratio when frames stay over 22 ms (`RES` in `stage.js`).
