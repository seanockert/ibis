# Environment

Street, fence, tufts, sky, backdrop, grade and the free play camera (`src/world/`, `src/demo.js`).

- Scale: the bird is 2 units, so 1 unit is about 35 cm. Keep the street in rough proportion, a little smaller than real, as in a cartoon: footpath 3, nature strip 6, road 14, bin 3 tall.
- Layout: strips along x (`STRIPS` in `street.js`). The road is on the -z side, so the default camera looks across the nature strip and the road. The bird starts on the nature strip.
- The ground is one mesh that follows the camera along x: `PROFILE` across z, straight along x. The road is `KERB.height` (0.2) lower than the rest, with a rolled kerb on each side: a flat gutter, then an S curve up to the top (less than `GROUND.stepUp`, so the bird walks up it). `PROFILE` is the one source of the heights: the mesh, the physics ground (`new Physics({ ground: PROFILE })`), the footprints (`fx.height`) and the tap to walk (`streetHeight()`). Its shader draws the strips in world space, with the toon band (`toonBand()`, so a slope that turns from the sun gets the shade) and a cool form tone where N.L is below `FORM`. A dark line on the kerb lip and a contact shade in the gutter make the drop read from the top side, where the roll is out of view.
- Footpaths: `PATH.height` (0.1) above the grass, with a round edge (an S curve `PATH.edge` wide) on each long side, in `PROFILE`: real geometry, as the kerb, because the bird steps on it and the props rest on it (about 16 triangles). The slab ends at the joints have a bevel in the shader only (`PATH.bevel`, `PATH.slope`): it tilts the normal toward the joint, so the end away from the sun takes the cool form tone. The round side of a slab that faces away from the sun is darker concrete (`pathSide`), not the lavender shade and the form tone: that read as a purple band. A path slope that faces the sun on the ground plane gets a light lip (7%), and the grass has a cool contact shade along each path edge (`PATH.contact`). Things that stand on a footpath take `streetHeight()`: the boxes and the cereal boxes (`ground` in their constructors), the joint tufts (`aGround`) and the footprints. A normal map or parallax mapping would cost more and add nothing at this size and view; keep real geometry for heights that play touches, and shading for the rest.
- `line(u, width)` in the ground shader takes its pixel width from `fwidth(u)`. For a u that wraps (the joints `jx`, the road dashes) pass `fwidth(p.x)`: at the wrap `fwidth(u)` jumps and draws a faint line in the middle of each slab.
- Ground depth: the ground draws first (`renderOrder -1`) and writes its depth `DEPTH_BIAS` (0.2) behind it along the view ray, with `gl_FragDepth`, so the outlines round the feet still show and a kerb hides what is below it on the road. The bias must be for each pixel: the triangles are 900 units long, so a bias on their corners is about zero near the bird. Less than 0.12 cuts the outline under the toes. The triangles also go from the lowest to the highest.
- Road: dark slate (`COLOURS.road` in `street.js`), so the light kerbs and the grass stand out.
- Grass (`street.js`): the sunny and lush patches have hard toon edges, one pixel wide. `GRASS` (`grassPatches()`, `grassColour()`) is shared by the ground and the tufts, so a tuft root has the colour of the ground under it. `#define FRAGMENT` before the chunk in a fragment shader turns on the pixel-wide edge (the vertex shader has no `fwidth`).
- Painted marks (`MARKS` in `street.js`): small tufts drawn flat in the ground shader in some cells of a grid, stretched so they look upright to the camera. `BLADES` (`tuft()`) gives the same shape as the 3D tufts. Each pixel tests 2 by 2 cells, so a mark must stay inside half a cell (the stretch is clamped at a low view). They are off past the patch fade (`near`).
- Pebbles (`PEBBLES` in `street.js`): round mounds in the ground shader near the strip edges (more nearer an edge, from the root's `edgeDistance()`), upright to the camera as the marks: light top, cool underside, small contact shadow. Not on the kerbs and the road: pale dots there read as road marks. Flat ovals on the ground were thin slivers at the play camera.
- Cracks (`CRACKS` in `street.js`): in some footpath slabs, a cartoon zigzag of three straight strokes from one side. Only footpath pixels run the code. Small tufts grow in some footpath joints (`TUFTS.joints`).
- Ground cost: the grass patches and the marks run only near a grass strip (`onGrass`, with a margin wide enough that the branch edge has no grass weight), the cracks only on the footpaths, the pebbles only near an edge and not on the kerbs and the road.
- `Upright` in the ground shader: the frame for the marks and the pebbles, made once in `main()`, outside any branch, because `fwidth()` in a branch is undefined in GLSL.
- Warm and cool: the lit ground colours are warm (yellow-green, peach), the shade colours cool (teal, lavender), far apart on purpose. `GRADE` adds to it: a cool lavender lift in the dark parts, a warm gain in the light parts.
- Tufts (`tufts.js`): one family, from thin grass (light, warm) to a few wide leaves (darker, cooler), from `aLeafy`. Each tuft is one flat green (a cooler one in the sun's shadow). A two-tone split per leaf looked too busy, and a light normal (ball or cylinder) split the tufts with a flat line across: do not go back to them. The root colour comes from `grassPatches()` in the vertex shader, once for each tuft. Placement by rules in `place()`: clusters (one big tuft, small ones round it, one kind) where a value noise is high, runs along the strip edges with gaps, and long thin grass round the foot of each fence post (`TUFTS.posts`, from `fencePosts()` in `fence.js`; not on the footpath). Wind: waves in world space and a small flutter, a shear of the quad top (`TUFTS.wind`). The birds push tufts aside (`uBirds`, up to 4, from `update(camera, dt, ibises)`). `alphaToCoverage` gives soft edges with the multisampled target of `grade.js`, with no sorting. Far tufts shrink to nothing at `TUFTS.fade`. Props: each prop has `solids()` (`{ body, half, round }`); the demo passes them to `update()`, and a tuft is no taller than the distance from its root to the nearest solid (`MAX_SOLIDS`), so no grass goes through a box. A new prop needs `solids()`. About 1300 tufts, one draw call. Cost: the tufts and the marks add about 0.1 ms GPU in free play at 1200x800 (measured under load: check again).
- The sky is a sphere that follows the camera, drawn after the opaque scene (`renderOrder 2`, after the bird outlines at 1 and before the anger lines at 3, which write no depth), on the far plane with `LessEqualDepth`: the depth test skips the pixels that the ground and the props cover. That saved about 0.35 ms in free play. An opaque object that writes no depth and has nothing behind it would be painted over by the sky: give it a `renderOrder` above 2. Clouds are circles in azimuth and elevation (`CLOUDS`, made with `hash()`). The far hills and trees are bands in the sky shader, with soft edges as a fake depth of field.
- Far ground fades to `HAZE` (pale sage), and the sky fills below the trees with the same colour, so the ground meets the horizon band. A blue haze read as a lake.
- Colours: picked from the concept art with a colour picker. Warm in the sun (yellow-green grass, peach footpath), cool in the shade (teal grass, lavender footpath). Tune against the concept, not by eye alone.
- Backdrop: the horizon must be close, or the street feels empty. Two rings of upright quads along rounded boxes (`RINGS`): a near green one just past the yards, and a taller, hazier one behind it. The shader cuts a canopy line (slow crowns plus round clumps) and shades the clumps. Transparent, soft edges, no depth write. The quads stop at the highest canopy, so the empty part costs nothing.
- Clouds: few (`CLOUDS.count` 5) and low, so the hills and the trees cover their feet. Each is a wide flat ellipse base, a dome off centre and a small bump on each side, white with a pale cool underside and a faint shade on the dome away from the sun. Keep them simple: puffy clouds of many circles looked busy. Cloud white stays a little below 1.0.
- Grade (`grade.js`): the target is 8-bit sRGB (`SRGB8_ALPHA8`, 4x MSAA): the hardware encodes on write and decodes on read, so the shaders see linear colour, with even steps in the dark. With `env` the canvas has no MSAA (`antialias: !env` in `demo.js`): it only gets the full-screen quad. Lifts dark parts toward lavender, warms light parts (not blue ones, or the sky goes teal), adds a little saturation and a warm vignette. It runs only with `env`, so the fixed views are unchanged. About 1 ms; the sky is about 1 ms more. The three.js `EffectComposer` with `UnrealBloomPass` cost about 5 ms: do not use it for a faint glow.
- Fixed views keep the plain background (`env=0`), so they still match `sdf.html`. `env=1` turns the street on for screenshots.
- Fence (`fence.js`, with `env`): the visible fence is `FENCE.out` (0.6) outside `PLAY`, on the yard grass 1.1 past the footpath (so the post tufts grow from grass, not the pavement), so a ball stops at the posts. Posts and rails: `toonMaterial()` and `outlineMaterial()` on instanced meshes (posts and rails cast shadows). Wire: one mesh of quads, a diamond pattern with `alphaToCoverage`, blended to a flat tint where a diamond is a few pixels (no shimmer); it does not cast a shadow (the shadow pass ignores the alpha). There is no fence at the two ends of the play area (a fence across the footpaths and the road read badly): the bounds stop the bird there, and the physics fence stops the props. With physics, each run is also a thin static wall on the `terrain` layer (as high as the posts), so heavy props (the bin, the boxes) can't go through. Size: posts 3.2 tall (a real 1.2 m fence is 3.4 units), above the flutter height (2.0).
- Demo camera: in free play, `follow()` moves the camera and its orbit centre together toward a point ahead of the bird (`CAMERA` in `demo.js`): a look-ahead along the velocity that builds and turns slowly (`lead`, `leadFreq`), critically damped springs for the follow (`follow`: loose, so the camera trails at a start and then overtakes), a soft dead zone (`slack`), a stiffer follow when the bird is far from the centre (`catchUp`), and up: the floor in full, part of a jump (`rise`) with a lead on the way up (`riseLead`). Orbit with a drag still works. Fixed views (`t`, `yaw`) do not follow. The orbit can't take the camera below `CAM_MIN_Y` (`maxPolarAngle` is set each frame from the distance).
- Plan for later phases: midground fences, letterbox, bushes and a street tree (toon meshes as in `props.js`, gobo noise for dappled shade); a backdrop of houses drawn to a half-size render target and blurred (depth of field); footprint and dust style for each surface.
